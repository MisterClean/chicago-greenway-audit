# Aldine and Cortland source review

Reviewed October 27, 2026. The accompanying `data/routes/review-evidence.json` is a scoped research batch, not a field approval. The city Bike Routes extract retrieved September 19, 2026 contains Aldine Clark–Broadway and Broadway–Lake Shore Drive, each labelled eastbound, and two separate Cortland rows: Damen–Ashland and Hamlin–Ridgeway, each labelled two-way. The `oneway_dir` field is the city motor-direction attribute; `br_oneway` and `br_ow_dir` describe bicycle-route direction. Neither set independently verifies current signs or motor permissions. Do not connect the geographically separated Cortland rows. The exact dataset request, retrieval timestamp and hash are recorded in the JSON. Its metadata identifies December 16, 2025 as the last row update.

## Aldine

The original [CDOT east long plot](https://www.44thward.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/LongPlot_-_School-Aldine__Roscoe_Clark_to_LSD.pdf) was re-inspected in full, including the Aldine/Broadway inset. Page 1 shows Aldine from Clark through Halsted to Broadway, then a northern offset to its eastern leg, which continues to Inner Lake Shore Drive. It depicts speed humps, curb extensions and a stop on the western Aldine approach; the inset depicts the northward Broadway jog and curb extensions. At Inner Lake Shore Drive the drawing depicts a bicycle crossing and a full-block curb extension. Neither humps, stop signs nor ordinary curb extensions alone sever motor continuity; the plan does not prove that a car can make each Broadway turn or use a lakefront underpass. An ordinary car may follow the designated jog if the individual motor turns and connector are permitted; it cannot use a bicycle-only link. PDF creation metadata is November 13, 2017; SHA-256 `b0a38b6474c52c699a88f840907338c7aff6a03700a5a9c38905260df53a3e6e`.

The [44th Ward project page](https://www.44thward.org/infrastructure/bike-pedestrian-infrastructure/school-aldine-roscoe-greenway/) reports on August 20, 2018 that curb extensions and speed humps were installed, and most striping was completed; the School/Clifton raised crosswalk was still in progress. The [August 8, 2018 Streetsblog report](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2018/08/08/eyes-on-the-street-roscoe-and-schoolaldine-greenway-begins-to-take-shape) documents curb extensions under construction at Aldine/Broadway and describes the northbound bicycle jog. These historical sources do not verify present signs or movements. Inspect both approaches and both turns at Broadway and current motor access at Inner Lake Shore Drive.

## Cortland: Damen–Ashland

[Active Transportation Alliance](https://activetransportation.org/blog/cdot-aldermen-share-plans-for-new-greenways/) reports the May 2016 CDOT public meeting with the 2nd and 32nd Ward aldermen for the proposed Bucktown Cortland and Wood greenways. A [December 2019 Streetsblog report of CDOT's meeting](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2019/12/05/a-cdot-show-and-tell-on-this-years-new-bikeways-and-whats-on-deck-for-2020) lists these greenways as accomplishments and says bicycle turn pockets were built at Damen and Marshfield. Those pockets support bicycle turns and do not establish car turns or barriers. Searches for an original detailed plan and ward project update did not produce an inspectable design; the CDOT project link in the advocacy article failed to load. The [32nd Ward infrastructure page](https://www.ward32.org/zoning-infrastructure) was inspected, but its Cortland Bridge item concerns a separate crossing east of the study segment and does not establish conditions here. Obtain a corridor plan and current signs at Damen, Marshfield, Ashland and intervening junctions.

## Cortland: Hamlin–Ridgeway

This short block is separate from the Bucktown greenway. A [City Council committee agenda](https://occprodstoragev1.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/meetingattachmentspublic/36065987-5e2c-48c4-93e3-237b39db438b.pdf), PDF page 5, lists a Ward 26 direct introduction, O2025-0019329, proposing a 20 mph limit on Cortland from Hamlin to Lawndale. The meeting date, passage and installation were not verified; a speed limit alone does not block through cars. The [June 2020 report on the temporary Slow Street](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2020/06/11/installation-of-slow-street-alternative-to-606-starts-tomorrow-with-wabansia-opening) describes a planned Ridgeway–Rockwell corridor, beginning at the eastern endpoint of this block. It neither proves a restriction west of Ridgeway nor a present restriction. Searches did not locate a block-specific CDOT greenway drawing or alderman project update. Verify the present ward boundary, obtain a block-specific plan and inspect both ends for signs and barriers.

A search for Cortland plans also surfaced the separate 2022 Encuentro Square planned development, including a right-of-way vacation south of Cortland, and the Cortland bridge project; neither establishes a through-car restriction on the reviewed block. The 2012 Ward 35 election map is historical and cannot settle present ward jurisdiction. These search leads require further investigation, rather than a current movement inference.

A further targeted search for the 2016 Wood/Cortland CDOT plan and Cortland/Marshfield design found contemporary reporting but no accessible original detailed plan graphic. The join recommendation for Aldine is limited to the documented designated bicycle route: in west-to-east orientation, the Clark–Broadway city row ends at the southern Broadway intersection and the Broadway–Lake Shore Drive row starts at the northern intersection. The intervening Broadway section and each vehicle turn remain unknown until separately checked; the geometric chord between city endpoints is a schematic gap, not verified pavement geometry.
