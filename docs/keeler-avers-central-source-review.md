# Keeler, Avers and Central Park preliminary source review

Reviewed 2026-10-27. This is a research handoff, not a route validation or a field survey. The [candidate records](../data/routes/review-evidence.json) bind all 12 exact Neighborhood Greenway source rows. Their way IDs are loose candidates: at least one OSM node lies within approximately five metres of the city polyline, using a local planar approximation. This test does **not** establish full overlap, endpoints, directional movements, intersection continuity or turn legality; the same way can appear against adjacent rows. Check exact node slices and both attachments before assignment. No different-name candidate has been validated. OSM ways were read from the staged snapshot, not a current ground inspection.

## Scope and limits

- Keeler: 33rd–25th Place, 25th Place–Ogden, Ogden–Cermak, Cermak–Arthington, Arthington–Harrison; separately Congress–Washington, Washington–West End, West End–Lake. Harrison–Congress across I-290 is not a city greenway row. Do not invent a connection. City attributes specify S on 33rd–25th Place, Ogden–Cermak, Arthington–Harrison, Congress–Washington and West End–Lake, with bicycle contraflow; '-' on the others is not an affirmative two-way motor designation.
- Avers: Armitage–Schubert (city N), Schubert–Diversey (city '-'). The city's `br_ow_dir` applies to bicycles and does not replace motor direction or OSM review.
- Central Park: Elston–alley (city S and bicycle contraflow), alley–Carmen (city '-'). The southern Central Park ordinary bicycle lanes elsewhere are not in this scope.

The [31st Street Corridor Study final report](https://rtams.org/sites/default/files/documents/2023-11/31st%20Street%20Corridor%20Study_Final%20Report_April%202023-compressed.pdf), April 2023, reports a Keeler neighborhood greenway installed in 2022 extending to 33rd Street. This is historical context for the South Lawndale portion; it is not a drawing or a current sign inspection. The search index exposed text; the PDF was not downloaded or visually inspected and should be verified directly before citing as reviewed primary evidence. [Streetsblog's 2024 Central Park field report](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2024/12/17/day-2-of-sbcs-bike-lane-week-2024-lincoln-square-irving-park-and-albany-park) describes contraflow bicycle access between Elston and Carmen; it is secondary historical context and does not establish current automobile access. Neither source is a detailed CDOT engineering sheet.
