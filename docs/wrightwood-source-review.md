# Wrightwood source review

Reviewed 2026-10-27 for an ordinary passenger car following the designated route. The editable, scoped observations and citations are in `data/routes/review-evidence.json`. These are snapshot inferences, not field approvals.

The city extract contains seven Wrightwood Neighborhood Greenway rows from Narragansett to Keeler. Include the two separately named connector rows at Cicero and Kostner when assembling the designated line: they trace the offsets between the Wrightwood arms. The city centerlines between Cicero and Kostner run near the median, approximately 18–20 m from each carriageway, and the city connector endpoints do not meet those centerlines. The designated carriageway and connector attachments remain unresolved. Streetsblog's [May 2025 account](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2025/05/16/an-intersection-redesign-advocates-pitched-years-ago-might-have-prevented-the-latest-serious-or-fatal-bike-crash-on-wrightwood-avenue) describes the north side as westbound and the south side as eastbound. The local OSM snapshot also maps these as separate one-way carriageways. Do not treat the parallel south carriageway or an off-route alternative as part of the designated route without evidence.

The city rows identify eastbound motor one-way direction on Narragansett–Austin and Central–Cicero. They also identify a bicycle contraflow; that exception does not apply to cars. The Austin–Central city row has `oneway_dir` `-`, which makes no cardinal assertion. The OSM snapshot is more specific: most sampled ways are eastbound one-way, with bicycle exception, and a short Central approach is tagged two-way. A bicycle 2W designation does not establish two-way motor traffic. Verify current signs and transitions.

Candidate OSM connector slices in the staged snapshot, listed south to north, are Cicero way 1153273868 (nodes 845883350, 157738013), followed by way 435326012 (157738013, 440912017); and Kostner way 1153273866 (261236499, 10670151977, 2266563851, 10670151973, 261236516, 2402691754, 440913744). These are topology candidates only; their endpoints do not resolve the gaps to the median-centered city lines, and they are not proof of legal turns. The direction and legality of every complete approach-to-departure movement at both offsets require review. A signal, stop sign, or speed hump by itself does not block through travel.

Searches on 2026-10-27 found the [31st Ward official site](https://www.ward31.org/) but no corridor-specific ward update or detailed accessible CDOT plan. A 2021 CDOT handout is described by [Streetsblog](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2021/07/29/cdot-announces-a-grid-of-new-bike-routes-for-belmont-cragin-and-hermosa), and its later [ribbon-cutting report](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2021/08/13/belmont-cragin-and-hermosa-celebrate-new-divvy-stations-bikeway-network) says contraflow lanes had been installed on Wrightwood, without establishing present signs or all movement permissions. A search result for the 2021 CDOT bike map returned 404 at its exact host and path, so no PDF graphics were inspected. These are dated search gaps, not evidence that no plan or update exists.

The exact dataset and extract URLs, hashes, retrieval dates, row identifiers, OSM objects, claims and review needs are retained in the JSON. The OSM checksum identifies the regional PBF snapshot, not the live object pages. Field verification should photograph current signs and physical treatments at segment transitions and both offsets, and check each turn on the designated line.

A targeted second pass retrieved the [Bikes for Belmont Cragin Evaluation Report](https://sharedusemobilitycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/BikesForBelmontCragin_v5.pdf), SHA-256 `6626acabdc69aaf2f998913a7d69e18e4ab6557e0d5e8f8b64b3395722e91e4c`. Its PDF creation metadata dates to September 12, 2023. Visual inspection of PDF page 9 (printed page 7), Figure 1.3, shows a CDOT-credited 2020 proposed network: a red planned line follows Wrightwood from Narragansett eastward and schematically jogs north at Cicero and south at Kostner. The neighboring text reports 2021 network-wide installations. The figure is a small conceptual network map, not a detailed engineering plan; it cannot establish exact carriageways, car movements, turn restrictions, or present conditions. The older [2023-hosted copy](https://sharedusemobilitycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/BikesForBelmontCragin_EnglishVersion.pdf) returned HTTP 404 on direct retrieval.

A standalone original 2021 CDOT handout was not located. The [older CDOT Streets for Cycling 2020 plan](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/cdot/bike/general/ChicagoStreetsforCycling2020.pdf), linked by Streetsblog for a pre-contraflow Drummond concept, returned HTTP 403; the corresponding `resources.chicago.gov` path returned 404. That older plan was not inspected and the alternative is not substituted for the designated route.

Additional ward searches examined the [36th Ward site](https://36thward.org/), [30th Ward plans](https://www.30thward.org/zoning/plans) and [newsletter archive](https://www.30thward.org/news-and-events/newsletter-archive), and [31st Ward site](https://www.ward31.org/). No scoped Wrightwood design or ward project update was located. The 30th Ward index lists newsletters from December 2024 through May 2026; this was not an exhaustive review of their contents. The 2021 network involved multiple wards, whose boundaries have since changed; exact present corridor-by-corridor assignments remain unverified. A detailed CDOT drawing and current field evidence remain open gaps.
